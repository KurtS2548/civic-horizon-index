Historical Perspective

Understanding How Priorities Change

America’s priorities have changed during periods of war, economic growth, social change, innovation, and national crisis.

1776

American Independence

The Declaration of Independence established the United States as a new nation and introduced enduring ideas about liberty, representation, and government.

1865

The Civil War Ends

The end of the Civil War began the difficult period of Reconstruction and major national debates about citizenship, equality, and reunification.

1929

The Great Depression

Economic collapse placed employment, financial security, banking reform, and government assistance at the center of national attention.

1941

United States Enters World War II

National defense, military service, industrial production, and international leadership became major priorities across the country.

1964

Civil Rights Act

The Civil Rights Act marked a major moment in the country’s continuing effort to address discrimination and protect equal rights.

1969

Moon Landing

The Apollo 11 mission demonstrated American scientific and technological achievement while inspiring new interest in education, research, and space exploration.

2001

September 11 Attacks

The September 11 attacks changed national discussions about security, foreign policy, emergency preparedness, and the balance between safety and civil liberties.

2008

Financial Crisis

The financial crisis renewed national concern about jobs, housing, banking regulation, retirement security, and economic stability.

2020

COVID-19 Pandemic

The pandemic placed public health, healthcare capacity, education, employment, and emergency response at the center of public attention.

2026

Civic Horizon Index

Civic Horizon Index begins building a public platform where Americans can share their priorities and explore how national concerns change over time.
