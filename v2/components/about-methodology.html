Methodology

How to interpret Civic Horizon results

Civic Horizon Index is a participation platform, not a traditional probability-based polling firm. That distinction is important when interpreting results.

01

Participation is voluntary

Results reflect people who choose to participate in Civic Horizon Index surveys and polls. Results should always be considered alongside the number of participants who contributed.

02

National Priorities use 1–10 ratings

Participants rate national issues from lower priority to highest priority. Average ratings are used to create the live National Priorities rankings.

03

Civic polls measure individual questions

Each civic poll measures responses to a specific question. Results apply only to that question and should be interpreted alongside its participation total.

04

Ongoing trackers measure current sentiment

Presidential polling and Civic Overview measures provide continuing snapshots of participant sentiment. Their results may change as new responses are collected.

05

Geographic results depend on eligible participation

State and other jurisdiction-specific results reflect eligible Civic Horizon participants associated with that geographic area.

06

Results change as participation grows

Rankings, percentages, and averages can change as new responses are submitted. Results represent a current snapshot rather than a fixed conclusion.

Important limitation

Unless a survey uses a scientifically designed sampling method, Civic Horizon Index results should not be described as representative of all Americans.
