01

Tariffs

Who pays them and how much money do they raise?

+
SIMPLE ANSWER

When goods enter the United States, the U.S. importer is responsible for paying the applicable import duty or tariff.

$210.3 billion in U.S. customs-duty revenue was reported for fiscal year 2025.

The U.S. Treasury reports that customs duties include tariff revenue. The comparable total for fiscal year 2024 was $76.4 billion.

Easy way to understand it: The foreign country does not directly write the U.S. government a tariff check. The importer pays the U.S. government. Businesses can then absorb some of that cost, negotiate with suppliers, or pass some of it along through prices.
Sources: U.S. Treasury · U.S. Customs and Border Protection ↗
02

Noncitizen Voting

How common is illegal noncitizen voting?

+
SIMPLE ANSWER

Noncitizens cannot legally vote in federal elections.

Illegal noncitizen voting can occur, but there is no complete federal database that tells us the exact number nationwide.

1,634 people were identified in a Georgia citizenship review as noncitizens who had attempted to register to vote.
0 ballots were cast by the people placed in Georgia's pending-citizenship group covered by that review.

Georgia's Secretary of State reported that the citizenship review used state records and the federal SAVE verification system. The people identified were not added to the voter rolls and were not permitted to vote.

Easy way to understand it: Noncitizen voting is illegal in federal elections. Real violations should be counted when they are found, but registration attempts are not the same thing as votes. We should not claim a nationwide percentage when no authoritative nationwide count exists.
Source: Georgia Secretary of State ↗
03

Social Security

Can noncitizens receive Social Security or SSI?

+
SIMPLE ANSWER

Yes, some lawfully present noncitizens can receive Social Security if they meet the normal eligibility requirements.

People who are unlawfully present in the United States cannot receive Social Security benefits under current law.

40 credits is the maximum number of work credits anyone can be required to have for a Social Security benefit. Retirement eligibility generally requires 40 credits.

In 2026, one Social Security credit is earned for each $1,890 in covered earnings, up to four credits in one year.

310,067 noncitizens received Supplemental Security Income, or SSI, in December 2025.
about 4.2% of the 7,392,201 total SSI recipients in December 2025 were noncitizens.

SSI and Social Security are not the same program. SSI has separate eligibility rules. The 310,067 figure refers to SSI recipients, not all Social Security recipients.

Easy way to understand it: Being a noncitizen does not automatically qualify someone for benefits. Immigration status and the eligibility rules of the specific program still apply.
Sources: Social Security Administration · SSA Work Credits · SSA 2025 SSI Statistics ↗
04

Medicare & Medicaid

How many people receive coverage, and can noncitizens qualify?

+
SIMPLE ANSWER

Medicaid and Medicare are different programs with different eligibility rules.

Some lawfully present noncitizens can qualify for Medicaid or CHIP, but immigration status, income, residency and other program rules still apply.

73.5 million people were enrolled in Medicaid or CHIP in May 2026.
66.4 million Medicaid
7.1 million CHIP

These are total program-enrollment numbers for everyone. They are not counts of immigrants or noncitizens.

5 years is the federal waiting period that applies to many qualified immigrants before they can enroll in Medicaid or CHIP.

There are important exceptions. States can cover certain lawfully residing children and pregnant people without making them wait five years.

Easy way to understand it: More than 73 million Americans are enrolled in Medicaid or CHIP. A person's noncitizen status does not automatically qualify them. Specific immigration and program eligibility rules still apply.
Sources: Medicaid.gov — May 2026 Enrollment · Medicaid.gov — Eligibility Rules ↗
05

Immigration & Taxes

Do undocumented immigrants pay U.S. taxes?

+
SIMPLE ANSWER

Yes. The IRS states that undocumented workers are subject to U.S. taxes despite their immigration status.

3.8 million federal tax returns filed for tax year 2022 contained at least one Individual Taxpayer Identification Number, or ITIN.
2.4+ million taxpayers filed a Form 1040 or 1040-NR for tax year 2023 with an ITIN holder listed as the primary taxpayer.

An ITIN is a tax-processing number for people who need a federal taxpayer identification number but are not eligible for a Social Security number.

Important: ITIN does not mean undocumented. ITIN holders include different types of resident and nonresident taxpayers. For that reason, these numbers cannot be used as a count of undocumented immigrants.

The IRS also makes clear that an ITIN does not provide immigration status, legal work authorization, Social Security benefits or the Earned Income Tax Credit.

Easy way to understand it: Undocumented status does not erase a person's tax obligations. But there is no IRS statistic that simply tells us, "this is exactly how much every undocumented immigrant paid in taxes."
Sources: IRS — Undocumented Workers · IRS — ITIN · IRS Taxpayer Advocate ITIN Data ↗
06

Vaccine Safety

How often are serious vaccine injury claims compensated?

+
SIMPLE ANSWER

Vaccines can cause side effects. Serious adverse reactions can occur, but federal data show compensated vaccine-injury claims are rare compared with the number of vaccine doses distributed.

5+ billion doses of vaccines covered by the federal Vaccine Injury Compensation Program were distributed in the United States from 2006 through 2023.
about 1 in 1 million doses distributed corresponded to approximately one person receiving compensation through the federal program.
13,948 claims adjudicated
10,193 compensated

Those figures apply to petitions filed for vaccines distributed during the 2006–2023 period covered by HRSA's January 2026 report.

Very important: Compensation does not always mean the government determined that a vaccine caused the reported injury. HRSA says approximately 60% of compensation awards result from negotiated settlements in which HHS did not conclude that the vaccine caused the alleged injury.

VAERS reports also should not be counted as proven vaccine injuries. A report can identify a possible safety signal that researchers investigate further, but the report itself does not establish cause.

Easy way to understand it: Vaccine injuries can occur, but a report, claim and proven injury are three different things. The denominator matters: billions of doses have been distributed.
Sources: U.S. Health Resources & Services Administration · CDC VAERS ↗
07

Inflation

Does lower inflation mean prices are going down?

+
SIMPLE ANSWER

Usually, no.

When inflation falls, prices are generally still rising — just at a slower rate.

3.4% U.S. consumer prices increased over the 12 months ending July 2026.
3.0% Food
3.2% Shelter
14.7% Energy
2.5% Core inflation

Core inflation is the change in prices excluding food and energy, which can move sharply from month to month.

Simple example $100 + 6% = $106 $106 + 3% = $109.18

Inflation dropped from 6% to 3%, but the price still went up.

Easy way to understand it: Lower inflation does not mean everything became cheaper. It usually means prices are increasing more slowly than before.
Source: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics — July 2026 CPI ↗
08

Gas Prices

What actually determines the price at the pump?

+
SIMPLE ANSWER

A president does not directly set the price displayed at a gas station.

Gasoline prices are made up mainly of crude oil costs, refining, distribution and marketing, and taxes.

$4.479 was the average regular gasoline price used in EIA's May 2026 gasoline-cost breakdown.
51.9% Crude oil
21.7% Refining
14.8% Distribution & marketing
11.5% Taxes

In that May 2026 breakdown, more than half of the pump price came from the cost of crude oil.

Easy way to understand it: Presidents and government policy can influence energy markets, but they do not simply choose the number shown on a gas pump. Oil markets and the cost of turning crude oil into delivered gasoline play major roles.
Source: U.S. Energy Information Administration ↗

Violent Crime

Is violent crime nationally rising or falling?

+
SIMPLE ANSWER

Nationally, violent crime fell substantially from 2024 to 2025.

-9.3% was the estimated decline in the national violent-crime rate from 2024 to 2025.
-18.1% Murder
-18.5% Robbery
-7.6% Rape
-7.2% Aggravated assault
4.1 per 100,000 was the estimated U.S. murder rate in 2025.

The FBI says the 2025 murder rate tied 1955 and 1956 for the lowest rate recorded in its national estimates.

17,000+ law-enforcement agencies submitted 2025 crime data, covering 96.2% of the U.S. population.
Easy way to understand it: Crime can rise in one community while falling in another. But when we talk about the United States as a whole, the FBI's 2025 data show violent crime moved downward.
Source: Federal Bureau of Investigation — 2025 Reported Crimes in the Nation ↗

Election Fraud

How common is election fraud in U.S. elections?

+
SIMPLE ANSWER

Election fraud is real and people can be investigated and prosecuted for election crimes.

But the United States does not maintain one nationwide database that counts every type of election fraud and divides it by every ballot cast.

158+ million ballots were counted in the 2024 U.S. general election.
nearly 65% of the U.S. citizen voting-age population participated in the 2024 general election.
100% response rate was received for the federal Election Administration and Voting Survey from all 50 states, the District of Columbia and five U.S. territories.

The Justice Department's Election Crimes Branch oversees federal election-crime matters including voting fraud, vote buying and absentee-ballot fraud.

Important: A criminal case proves that an election crime can occur. It does not automatically establish that fraud was widespread throughout an election or that enough fraudulent votes existed to change an outcome.
Easy way to understand it: More than 158 million ballots were counted in 2024. Election crimes should be investigated whenever evidence exists, but there is no honest nationwide fraud percentage we can place here because the federal government does not publish one comprehensive statistic covering every form of election fraud.
Sources: U.S. Election Assistance Commission — 2024 EAVS · U.S. Department of Justice — Election Crimes Branch ↗
