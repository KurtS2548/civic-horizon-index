COMMON QUESTIONS

01

Tariffs

Who actually pays tariffs?

+
SIMPLE ANSWER

The U.S. importer is responsible for paying applicable tariffs, duties and fees when goods are imported into the United States.

The importer may then raise prices, absorb some of the cost, or negotiate a lower price from its supplier.

Bottom line: A tariff is not simply a foreign government sending money to the United States.
Source: U.S. Customs and Border Protection ↗
02

Noncitizen Voting

Can noncitizens legally vote?

+
SIMPLE ANSWER

Noncitizens cannot legally register or vote in federal elections.

Illegal voting does happen and people can be prosecuted for it. Individual cases, however, do not tell us how common it is nationwide.

3 noncitizens pleaded guilty in three separate federal cases in Florida in June 2026.
Bottom line: Federal elections require U.S. citizenship. Documented violations exist.
Source: U.S. Department of Justice ↗
03

Social Security

Can noncitizens receive benefits?

+
SIMPLE ANSWER

Yes. Some lawfully present noncitizens can receive Social Security benefits.

They still have to meet the program's normal eligibility requirements, including enough work credits when required.

Bottom line: Citizenship is not always required, but lawful presence and normal eligibility rules matter.
Source: Social Security Administration ↗
04

Medicare & Medicaid

Who can actually qualify?

+
SIMPLE ANSWER

Medicare and Medicaid are different programs and have different eligibility rules.

Some qualified noncitizens can receive Medicaid if they meet their state's income, residency and other eligibility requirements.

5 years is the general Medicaid and CHIP waiting period for many qualified noncitizens. Important exceptions exist.

Medicare has separate eligibility and immigration-status rules. The requirements should not be confused with Medicaid's five-year rule.

Sources: HealthCare.gov · Medicare.gov ↗
05

Immigration & Taxes

Do undocumented immigrants pay taxes?

+
SIMPLE ANSWER

Yes. Immigration status and federal tax obligations are separate issues.

People who are not eligible for a Social Security number can use an Individual Taxpayer Identification Number, or ITIN, when they have a federal tax filing or reporting requirement.

Important: An ITIN does not provide legal immigration status, work authorization or Social Security benefits.
Source: Internal Revenue Service ↗
06

Vaccines

What do vaccine reports actually mean?

+
SIMPLE ANSWER

Vaccines can cause side effects, and vaccine safety continues to be monitored after approval.

A report submitted to VAERS does not by itself prove that a vaccine caused the reported health problem.

Bottom line: VAERS is an early-warning system. Possible safety signals require further scientific investigation.
Source: CDC Vaccine Safety Systems ↗
07

Inflation

Does lower inflation mean lower prices?

+
SIMPLE ANSWER

Usually, no.

Lower inflation normally means prices are rising more slowly. It does not necessarily mean prices are falling.

3.4% U.S. consumer-price inflation over the 12 months ending July 2026.
Simple example $100 + 6% = $106 $106 + 3% = $109.18

Inflation fell from 6% to 3%, but the price still increased.

Source: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics ↗
08

Gas Prices

Does the president set gas prices?

+
SIMPLE ANSWER

No. A president does not directly set the price shown at the gas pump.

Gas prices are affected by crude oil prices, refining, distribution, marketing and taxes.

51.9% of the average U.S. gasoline price in May 2026 came from the crude-oil component.

Government policies can influence energy markets, but they are only one part of gasoline pricing.

Source: U.S. Energy Information Administration ↗
09

Crime

Is violent crime rising or falling?

+
SIMPLE ANSWER

Nationally, violent crime fell significantly from 2024 to 2025.

-9.3% Violent crime
-18.1% Murder
-18.5% Robbery
-7.2% Aggravated assault

National trends do not mean crime changed the same way in every city or state.

Source: Federal Bureau of Investigation ↗
10

Election Fraud

Does election fraud happen?

+
SIMPLE ANSWER

Yes. Election crimes happen and people are prosecuted for them.

Federal election crimes can include voting fraud, vote buying, absentee ballot fraud and other election offenses.

Important: A documented case proves that the specific violation occurred. It does not by itself prove that fraud was widespread throughout an election.

There is no single federal statistic that counts every type of election fraud nationwide.

Source: DOJ Election Crimes Branch ↗
