First Things First

Constitutional rights usually limit government

The Constitution mainly controls what federal, state, and local governments may do.

A private business, employer, website, or organization is usually not the government, so constitutional rules do not always apply to them in the same way.

Constitutional rights are powerful, but many are not unlimited. The exact rule can depend on the situation.

First Amendment Freedom of Speech
+

Can you criticize the government?

Yes. The First Amendment provides strong protection for people expressing opinions about government, politics, public officials, and public issues.

The government generally cannot punish you simply because it dislikes your political opinion.

Everyday example

You can publicly disagree with the President, a governor, a mayor, members of Congress, or other government officials.

Important

Freedom of speech does not protect every type of speech in every situation. Some legal limits can apply.

First Amendment Peaceful Protest & Assembly
+

Do you have a right to protest?

The First Amendment protects the right of people to assemble peacefully.

People can gather to support a cause, criticize government decisions, hold rallies, and ask elected officials to make changes.

Everyday example

A group may organize a peaceful demonstration about a government policy.

Important

Governments may sometimes enforce neutral rules involving permits, traffic, safety, location, and timing.

First Amendment Freedom of Religion
+

Can the government choose your religion?

The First Amendment protects religious freedom.

Government generally cannot establish an official religion or force people to follow a particular religious belief.

People may practice a religion, change religions, or choose not to practice a religion.

Fourth Amendment Privacy & Government Searches
+

Can the government search you or your property?

The Fourth Amendment protects people from unreasonable searches and seizures by government.

Police generally need a valid warrant before searching a person's home.

A warrant is normally approved by a judge and supported by probable cause.

Important

A warrant is not required in every situation. Whether a search is constitutional can depend on the circumstances.

Privacy Your Phone & Digital Life
+

Can constitutional protections apply to technology?

Yes. Fourth Amendment protections can apply to modern technology and digital information.

Courts have considered how constitutional protections apply to cell phones and other digital information.

Government and private companies are different

The Fourth Amendment limits government action. Private companies can be governed by different laws, contracts, and privacy policies.

Fifth & Fourteenth Amendments Due Process
+

Does government have to follow fair procedures?

The Constitution says government cannot deprive a person of life, liberty, or property without due process of law.

In simple terms, government must follow legally required procedures before taking certain actions against someone.

Simple example

Depending on the situation, due process may require notice and a meaningful opportunity to respond.

Criminal Justice Rights When Accused of a Crime
+

Does an accused person still have rights?

Yes. Being accused of a crime does not mean a person loses constitutional protections.

Depending on the situation, protections can include the right against being forced to testify against yourself, the right to a lawyer, and the right to a speedy and public trial.

An accusation is not proof of guilt

In a criminal case, the government has the burden of proving guilt beyond a reasonable doubt.

Voting Rights Elections & Voting
+

Does the Constitution protect voting rights?

Yes. Several constitutional amendments protect against certain forms of discrimination in voting.

Voting rights cannot be denied because of race, because of sex, or — for citizens age 18 or older — because of age.

Elections still have rules

States administer elections and may establish voting procedures, but those rules must comply with the Constitution and applicable federal law.

Public Schools Student Rights
+

Do students have constitutional rights?

Yes. Public schools are government institutions, so students can have constitutional protections involving speech, religion, searches, and due process.

School is a special setting

Students do not always have exactly the same freedom at school that adults have in other public settings.

Schools may have additional authority to maintain safety, order, and an effective learning environment.

Fourteenth Amendment Equal Protection
+

Does government have to treat people equally?

The Fourteenth Amendment says states cannot deny any person the equal protection of the laws.

This means government cannot treat people differently in ways that violate the Constitution.

Equal does not always mean identical

Government sometimes makes legal distinctions. Whether a distinction is constitutional depends on the reason for it and the rights involved.

Important Difference Government vs. Private Organizations
+

Does the Constitution control private businesses?

Usually, constitutional protections restrict government action rather than actions taken by private businesses or organizations.

Private organizations still must follow other laws that apply to them.

A useful question

When thinking about a constitutional right, first ask: Is the government involved?

The Big Picture

Your rights protect you, but the details matter.

The Constitution protects important freedoms and limits government power.

How a particular right applies can depend on who is acting, what happened, where it happened, and the specific circumstances.
