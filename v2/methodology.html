Methodology

How Civic Horizon results work

Civic Horizon Index is a civic participation platform. This page explains how participation is collected, how results are calculated, and what those results do and do not represent.

Core Principle Participation should be transparent, understandable, and difficult to manipulate. Results should always be viewed alongside participation totals and the rules used to collect them.

Understanding the Data

What Civic Horizon results represent

Civic Horizon results reflect people who voluntarily choose to participate on the platform.

01

Participation is voluntary

Civic Horizon does not randomly select participants from the U.S. population. Results reflect the people who choose to create an account and participate.

02

Participation totals provide context

Percentages, rankings, and averages should always be considered alongside the number of participants who contributed to them.

03

Civic Horizon is not automatically a scientific poll

Unless a survey specifically uses a scientifically designed sampling method, Civic Horizon results should not be described as representative of all Americans.

Participation

Who can participate

Civic Horizon uses participant accounts and eligibility checks to help protect the integrity of results.

Accounts

Participation requires an account

Participants must sign in through their Civic Horizon account before submitting votes or survey responses.

Verification

Verified participation

Civic Horizon may require verification steps, including verified email and required profile information, before participation is enabled.

Geography

Geographic eligibility matters

State and district-specific participation is limited to participants whose verified geographic information matches the relevant jurisdiction.

National Priorities

How national priority rankings are calculated

Participants rate each tracked national issue using the same 1–10 priority scale.

01

Each issue receives a 1–10 rating

A rating of 1 represents a lower priority, while a rating of 10 represents the highest priority on the Civic Horizon scale.

02

Average ratings determine rank

Civic Horizon calculates the average participant rating for each issue and orders the National Priorities ranking from highest average to lowest.

03

Rankings can change

National rankings update as eligible participants submit new responses during the current participation period.

Civic Polls

How individual poll results work

Civic polls measure responses to a specific question and are interpreted independently.

Question

Each poll stands on its own

A poll result applies only to the question shown and should not be used to infer views on unrelated issues.

Results

Percentages come from recorded responses

The percentage for each answer choice is calculated from the valid responses submitted to that poll.

Context

Vote totals matter

Civic Horizon displays participation totals so a result with a small number of responses is not mistaken for broad public consensus.

Ongoing Trackers

Presidential polling and Civic Overview

Some Civic Horizon measures are designed to provide recurring snapshots rather than one-time results.

Presidential Poll

Presidential job approval

Participants assess the current president's job performance using a consistent approval scale. Results are displayed as a current participant snapshot.

Country Direction

Right direction or wrong track

Country Direction measures whether participants believe the country is generally moving in the right direction or on the wrong track.

National Confidence

Confidence across major institutions

National Confidence summarizes participant ratings across the civic and institutional categories measured by Civic Horizon.

Participation Periods

Recurring surveys use defined voting periods

Different Civic Horizon trackers may reopen on different schedules so participants can provide updated views over time.

Weekly

Weekly trackers

Some sentiment trackers allow one eligible response per participant during each weekly participation period.

Monthly

Monthly surveys

Some surveys allow one eligible response per participant during each monthly participation period.

Automatic Reset

New periods reopen automatically

When a new participation period begins, eligible participants can respond again without requiring a manual reset of earlier results.

Vote Integrity

Protecting participation from duplicate voting

Civic Horizon uses participation records and eligibility controls to reduce duplicate or unauthorized responses.

01

One eligible response per voting period

Recurring trackers record whether an authenticated participant has already responded during the current participation period.

02

Individual civic polls use participation locks

Civic Horizon records whether an eligible account has already participated in an individual civic poll so the same account cannot repeatedly vote in that question.

03

Public results do not need to expose account identity

Participation controls can use private account records while public result totals remain separated from personally identifying account information.

Geographic Results

State and district participation

Geographic participation is designed to keep local results tied to the people eligible to participate in that jurisdiction.

State

State participation

State-specific questions and approval measurements are limited to eligible participants associated with that state.

District

Congressional participation

U.S. representative participation is limited to participants whose geographic information can be matched to the appropriate congressional district.

Read Access

Results may remain visible outside the jurisdiction

Participants may be able to view civic information and results outside their own geographic area even when they are not eligible to vote there.

Important Limitations

What Civic Horizon results should not be used to claim

Civic Horizon participation data can provide useful insight into the views of people using the platform, but participation alone does not establish that those views represent the entire population of a state, district, or the United States.

Results should not be described as scientifically representative unless the survey methodology specifically supports that conclusion.
