Understand Your Government

Federal vs. State vs. Local — Who Does What?

Different levels of government handle different responsibilities.

Choose a level below to see what it usually controls and who you should look to when you want something changed.

The Simple Version

Not every problem belongs to Washington

The federal government handles national responsibilities. State governments handle many statewide responsibilities. Counties, cities, towns, and school districts handle many issues closer to home.

Some responsibilities are shared, so more than one level of government can be involved at the same time.

Taking Action

Who should I contact?

Start with the level of government that is most directly responsible for your concern.

National Issue Contact Federal Officials
+

For federal laws, national policies, or other federal issues, you may want to contact your members of Congress or the appropriate federal agency.

Statewide Issue Contact State Officials
+

For state laws and statewide policies, you may want to contact your state legislators, governor's office, or the appropriate state agency.

County Issue Contact County Government
+

For county services, records, county roads, or other county responsibilities, start with the appropriate county office or your county's elected governing body.

Local Issue Contact Your City or Town
+

For local roads, parks, zoning, trash, permits, or other municipal services, start with your city or town government.

School Issue Contact Your School District
+

For many local public-school issues, start with the school, district administration, or local school board.

Remember

Some education policies are controlled by state or federal law, so the local school district may not control every issue.

The Big Picture

Start with one simple question:

Who actually has the power to make this decision?

Knowing which level of government controls an issue makes it easier to understand government — and easier to know where your voice should be heard.
