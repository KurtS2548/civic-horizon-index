Neutrality Statement

Civic participation without political persuasion

Civic Horizon Index is designed to help people participate, learn, and explore civic information without promoting a political party, candidate, ideology, or preferred outcome.

Our Commitment Inform people clearly and let them reach their own conclusions. Neutrality does not mean avoiding facts. It means separating factual information from political persuasion.

Core Commitment

Civic Horizon does not tell participants what to think

The platform exists to provide tools for participation, information, and civic understanding. Political conclusions belong to the participant.

01

No political endorsement

Civic Horizon Index does not endorse political parties, candidates, elected officials, campaigns, or ideological movements.

02

No preferred poll outcome

Polls and surveys are not designed to produce a preferred political result. Participant responses determine the results displayed on the platform.

03

No political scoring of people

Civic Horizon does not assign ideological scores to participants or label people based on how they answer civic questions.

Question Design

Questions should be clear and balanced

Poll wording can influence responses, so Civic Horizon aims to avoid emotionally loaded, misleading, or unnecessarily partisan language.

Clarity

Use understandable language

Questions should be written so participants can understand what is being asked without needing specialized political knowledge.

Balance

Avoid steering responses

Question wording should avoid suggesting that one response is more reasonable, patriotic, moral, or acceptable than another.

Consistency

Keep recurring trackers stable

Ongoing measures should use consistent wording whenever practical so changes over time are easier to interpret.

Facts and Opinion

Participant views and factual information are different

Civic Horizon presents both participant opinion and civic information, but the two should not be confused.

Participant Results

Poll results show what participants think

Rankings, approval ratings, civic polls, and sentiment measures represent responses submitted by Civic Horizon participants.

Factual Information

Civic facts should rely on reliable sources

Information about public officials, government institutions, voting records, and other civic facts should use official or reliable public sources whenever possible.

Separation

Facts should not be presented as endorsement

Showing an official record, statistic, or public source does not mean Civic Horizon supports or opposes the person or policy being described.

Public Officials

The same standards should apply regardless of party

Civic Horizon should present comparable public information using the same general standards whether an official is Republican, Democratic, independent, or affiliated with another party.

01

Approval measures use the same scale

Elected-official approval questions should use consistent response choices regardless of the official's political affiliation.

02

Comparable facts should use comparable standards

When Civic Horizon displays information about public officials, similar categories of information should be handled consistently across parties.

03

Participant approval is not Civic Horizon's opinion

Approval percentages reflect participant responses. They do not represent an endorsement or judgment by Civic Horizon Index.

Results

Results should be shown without exaggeration

Civic Horizon should present participant data honestly, including the context and limitations needed to understand it.

Totals

Show participation counts

Percentages should be displayed alongside response totals whenever practical so users can judge the size of the participating group.

Limitations

Do not overstate representativeness

Voluntary Civic Horizon participation should not be presented as scientifically representative of the entire population unless the methodology supports that claim.

Interpretation

Let the numbers speak for themselves

Civic Horizon should avoid editorial language that describes a result as good, bad, surprising, embarrassing, or politically desirable.

Accountability

Neutrality is a standard to maintain, not a claim of perfection

Civic Horizon Index aims to apply these principles consistently as the platform grows. Questions, descriptions, data displays, and methodology may be reviewed and improved when wording or presentation could create unnecessary bias or confusion.

Maintaining trust requires being willing to correct mistakes and improve the platform when legitimate concerns are identified.
