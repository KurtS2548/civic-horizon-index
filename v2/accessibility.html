Accessibility

Civic participation should be usable by everyone

Civic Horizon Index is designed to make civic information, participation, and navigation as clear and accessible as practical across devices and different ways of interacting with the web.

Our Goal Make important civic information easier to read, understand, navigate, and use. Accessibility is treated as an ongoing product responsibility rather than a one-time checklist.

Our Approach

Clear design is part of accessibility

Civic Horizon aims to reduce unnecessary complexity so participants can focus on the information and actions that matter.

Readability

Clear text and strong contrast

The platform aims to use readable typography, understandable labels, and sufficient visual contrast throughout major participant-facing pages.

Navigation

Consistent page structure

Shared navigation, headings, buttons, and page patterns are intended to make it easier for participants to understand where they are and where they can go next.

Simplicity

Reduce unnecessary steps

Participant-facing tasks should require as few confusing steps as practical, especially for voting, account management, and civic exploration.

Interaction

Support more than one way to use the site

Civic Horizon aims to support keyboard navigation and assistive technologies throughout major participant-facing experiences.

01

Keyboard access

Important interactive controls should be usable without requiring a mouse whenever practical.

02

Meaningful labels

Forms, buttons, navigation controls, and interactive content should use meaningful labels and accessible names.

03

Logical structure

Pages should use headings and semantic structure that help both visual users and assistive technologies understand the organization of the page.

Mobile Access

Civic Horizon should work across screen sizes

Many participants will use phones and tablets, so major platform experiences are designed to adapt to smaller screens.

Responsive Design

Layouts adapt to smaller screens

Cards, navigation, forms, results, and civic information should reorganize cleanly rather than requiring a desktop-sized display.

Touch Targets

Controls should be easy to select

Buttons and interactive controls should be sized and spaced to reduce accidental taps on touch devices.

Alternatives

Complex interactions may use simpler mobile options

When an interaction such as a large map is not practical on a small screen, Civic Horizon may provide an alternate selector or simplified mobile experience.

Plain Language

Civic information should be understandable

Accessibility also means reducing unnecessary jargon and explaining unfamiliar civic concepts clearly.

01

Explain civic terms

When specialized government or policy language is necessary, Civic Horizon should provide enough context for ordinary participants to understand it.

02

Avoid unnecessary technical language

Participant-facing pages should favor straightforward explanations over internal technical or administrative terminology.

03

Make important limitations easy to find

Methodology, participation totals, and other important context should be written so participants can understand what the information does and does not mean.

Ongoing Improvement

Accessibility will continue to improve as the platform grows

Civic Horizon Index aims to identify and correct accessibility problems as they are discovered. New pages and features should be reviewed for readability, keyboard access, responsive behavior, and understandable interaction.

Participants who encounter an accessibility problem can use the Contact page to report it.
