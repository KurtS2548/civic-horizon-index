Understand Your Government

Amendments 11–27 — In Simple Terms

The Constitution has 27 amendments in total. The first 10 are called the Bill of Rights.

These next 17 amendments changed important parts of American government and expanded important constitutional protections over time.

Amendment 11

Limits on Lawsuits Against States

In simple terms

The Eleventh Amendment limits certain lawsuits against states in federal court.

In simple terms, states have some protection from being sued in federal court without their consent.

Amendment 12

How the President and Vice President Are Elected

In simple terms

The Twelfth Amendment changed how the Electoral College votes for President and Vice President.

Electors now cast separate votes for President and Vice President instead of using the older system.

Amendment 13

Slavery Was Abolished

In simple terms

The Thirteenth Amendment abolished slavery and involuntary servitude in the United States, except as punishment for a crime after conviction.

Why it matters

This amendment made the end of slavery part of the Constitution itself.

Amendment 14

Citizenship, Due Process, and Equal Protection

In simple terms

The Fourteenth Amendment established national citizenship for people born or naturalized in the United States.

It also says states cannot deprive people of life, liberty, or property without due process of law, and cannot deny people equal protection of the laws.

Why it matters

This amendment is one of the most important parts of the Constitution for modern civil rights and individual protections.

Amendment 15

Voting Rights and Race

In simple terms

The Fifteenth Amendment says the right to vote cannot be denied by the United States or a state because of race, color, or previous condition of servitude.

Why it matters

This amendment added an important constitutional protection for voting rights.

Amendment 16

Federal Income Taxes

In simple terms

The Sixteenth Amendment gives Congress the power to collect a federal income tax without dividing that tax among the states based on population.

Why it matters

Income taxes became an important source of money used to fund the federal government.

Amendment 17

Voters Directly Elect U.S. Senators

In simple terms

The Seventeenth Amendment changed how United States senators are chosen.

Before this amendment, state legislatures generally selected U.S. senators.

The amendment changed the system so voters in each state directly elect their senators.

Amendment 18

Prohibition

In simple terms

The Eighteenth Amendment created Prohibition, banning the manufacture, sale, and transportation of alcoholic beverages for drinking.

It did not remain permanent.

What happened later?

The Twenty-First Amendment later repealed the Eighteenth Amendment and ended national Prohibition.

Amendment 19

Voting Rights and Sex

In simple terms

The Nineteenth Amendment says the right to vote cannot be denied by the United States or a state because of sex.

Why it matters

This amendment constitutionally protected women from being denied the right to vote because they are women.

Amendment 20

When Presidential and Congressional Terms Begin

In simple terms

The Twentieth Amendment changed when the terms of the President, Vice President, and members of Congress begin and end.

Presidential and vice-presidential terms begin on January 20.

Congressional terms begin on January 3.

Why was this changed?

The amendment shortened the long period between an election and the time newly elected federal officials take office.

Amendment 21

Prohibition Was Repealed

In simple terms

The Twenty-First Amendment repealed the Eighteenth Amendment and ended national Prohibition.

This meant the Constitution no longer prohibited the manufacture, sale, and transportation of alcoholic beverages nationwide.

An unusual amendment

The Twenty-First Amendment is the only amendment that was adopted specifically to repeal another constitutional amendment.

Amendment 22

Presidential Term Limits

In simple terms

The Twenty-Second Amendment generally limits a person to being elected President twice.

Special rules apply when someone becomes President during another President's term, so the exact maximum amount of time a person may serve can depend on how they first entered the office.

The key idea

The Constitution now places a limit on how many times a person can be elected President.

Amendment 23

Washington, D.C. and Presidential Elections

In simple terms

The Twenty-Third Amendment allows Washington, D.C. to participate in presidential elections through the Electoral College.

The District receives electoral votes, although it is not a state.

Why it matters

Residents of Washington, D.C. gained a constitutional role in choosing the President and Vice President.

Amendment 24

No Poll Tax in Federal Elections

In simple terms

The Twenty-Fourth Amendment says people cannot be required to pay a poll tax or other tax in order to vote in federal elections.

Why it matters

Paying a tax cannot be made a condition for exercising the right to vote in the federal elections covered by this amendment.

Amendment 25

What Happens If a President Cannot Serve?

In simple terms

The Twenty-Fifth Amendment explains what happens when a President dies, resigns, is removed from office, or is unable to carry out the duties of the presidency.

It confirms that the Vice President becomes President when the presidency becomes vacant.

It also provides a process for filling a vacant vice presidency and procedures for situations when a President is unable to perform the duties of the office.

Why it matters

The amendment helps make sure there is a clear process for presidential leadership when serious questions arise about who can perform the duties of the presidency.

Amendment 26

Voting at Age 18

In simple terms

The Twenty-Sixth Amendment says the right to vote of United States citizens who are 18 years old or older cannot be denied or limited because of age.

Why it matters

The amendment established constitutional protection against denying adult citizens the right to vote simply because they are younger than 21.

Amendment 27

Congressional Pay Changes

In simple terms

The Twenty-Seventh Amendment says a law changing the pay of members of Congress cannot take effect until after the next election for the House of Representatives.

The key idea

Congress can pass legislation changing congressional pay, but the change cannot take effect immediately.

A House election must occur first.

The Big Picture

What should you remember?

The Constitution has changed as the country has changed. Amendments allow important changes to become part of the Constitution itself.

01

Rights expanded

Several amendments expanded constitutional protections involving citizenship and voting.

02

Government changed

Amendments changed important parts of government, including Senate elections, presidential elections, presidential terms, and presidential succession.

03

Major national changes became constitutional

Amendments abolished slavery, protected voting rights in important ways, and changed other major parts of American law and government.

04

Amendments can change earlier rules

The Constitution can be changed again. The Twenty-First Amendment is a clear example: it repealed the Eighteenth Amendment.

The simplest way to think about it

The Constitution was designed to be changeable, but not easy to change.

The amendment process allows the country to make lasting constitutional changes when there is broad enough agreement to do so.
