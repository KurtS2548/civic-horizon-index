Category 1 Government & Constitution
+

Constitution

The highest law of the United States. It creates the basic structure of the federal government, divides government power, and protects certain rights.

Amendment

An official change or addition to the Constitution.

Bill of Rights

The first 10 amendments to the U.S. Constitution. They protect important individual rights and liberties.

Federal Government

The national government of the United States, made up of the legislative, executive, and judicial branches.

Separation of Powers

The division of federal government authority among Congress, the President, and the federal courts.

Checks and Balances

The system that gives each branch of the federal government ways to limit or influence the powers of the other branches.

Federalism

The division of governmental authority between the national government and state governments.

Constitutional

Consistent with or permitted by the Constitution.

Unconstitutional

Contrary to the Constitution. Courts can rule that government actions or laws are unconstitutional.

Civil Liberty

A freedom protected from improper government interference, such as freedoms involving speech or religion.

Category 2 Congress & Laws
+

Congress

The legislative branch of the federal government. Congress consists of the House of Representatives and the Senate.

House of Representatives

One chamber of Congress. Representation is based on each state's population.

Senate

One chamber of Congress. Each state has two senators.

Bill

A proposal for legislation being considered by Congress or another legislative body.

Law

A rule established through a government's legal process and enforceable by government.

Committee

A smaller group of legislators that studies issues, considers legislation, and performs other congressional work.

Appropriation

Legal authority provided by Congress for federal agencies to spend money for specified purposes.

Veto

The President's rejection of legislation passed by Congress. Congress can override a regular presidential veto with the required two-thirds vote in both chambers.

Filibuster

A Senate practice used to delay or block action on a matter. Senate rules provide procedures for ending debate in many situations.

Bipartisan

Involving cooperation or support from members of two political parties.

Category 3 President & Executive Branch
+

President

The head of the executive branch of the federal government.

Executive Branch

The branch of federal government responsible for carrying out and enforcing federal laws.

Vice President

The official who serves directly below the President and can become President if the presidency becomes vacant.

Cabinet

A group of senior executive-branch officials who advise the President and lead major federal departments.

Executive Order

A written directive from the President that directs executive-branch operations within presidential legal authority.

Federal Agency

A government organization responsible for carrying out specific federal laws, programs, or responsibilities.

Commander in Chief

The President's constitutional role as the head of the U.S. armed forces.

Impeachment

The constitutional process in which the House of Representatives formally accuses certain federal officials of serious misconduct.

Removal from Office

After impeachment by the House, the Senate can vote to convict and remove an official from office.

Pardon

An act of executive clemency that can forgive a federal criminal offense.

Category 4 Courts & Justice
+

Judicial Branch

The branch of government that includes the courts and interprets laws in cases brought before them.

Supreme Court

The highest court in the federal judicial system.

Federal Court

A court that is part of the federal judicial system.

Judge

A public official who oversees court proceedings and applies the law.

Judicial Review

The power of courts to determine whether government actions or laws are consistent with the Constitution.

Plaintiff

A person or organization that brings a civil lawsuit.

Defendant

A person or organization against whom a legal case is brought.

Appeal

A request for a higher court to review a lower court's decision.

Due Process

The constitutional requirement that government follow legally required procedures before taking certain actions affecting life, liberty, or property.

Equal Protection

A constitutional principle requiring states to provide equal protection of the laws.

Precedent

A previous court decision that can guide how courts handle similar legal questions later.

Constitutionality

The question of whether a law or government action is consistent with the Constitution.

Category 5 Elections & Voting
+

General Election

An election in which voters choose among candidates for public office or decide ballot questions.

Primary Election

An election used to help political parties choose their candidates for a later general election.

Ballot

The official method voters use to record their choices in an election.

Ballot Measure

A proposed law, constitutional change, or other public question placed directly before voters.

Voter Registration

The process of registering with election officials so a person can vote when required by state law.

Absentee Voting

A method that allows an eligible voter to cast a ballot without voting in person at a polling place on Election Day.

Early Voting

Voting that takes place before Election Day during a period authorized by state law.

Popular Vote

The total number of votes cast directly for a candidate or choice by voters.

Electoral College

The constitutional process used to formally elect the President and Vice President of the United States.

Electoral Vote

A vote cast by a presidential elector as part of the Electoral College process.

Turnout

A measure of how many people participate in an election.

Incumbent

A person who currently holds the public office being contested in an election.

Category 6 Taxes & Government Money
+

Tax

Money collected by government under law to help pay for public programs, services, and obligations.

Revenue

Money received by government, including money collected through taxes and other sources.

Federal Budget

The federal government's financial plan involving expected revenue and government spending.

Federal Spending

Money paid by the federal government for programs, services, benefits, operations, interest, and other legal obligations.

Mandatory Spending

Federal spending generally determined by laws that establish eligibility requirements or payment formulas.

Discretionary Spending

Federal spending generally decided through the annual appropriations process.

Budget Deficit

The amount by which government spending exceeds government revenue during a particular period.

Budget Surplus

The amount by which government revenue exceeds government spending during a particular period.

National Debt

The accumulated amount the federal government owes as a result of borrowing.

Debt Ceiling

The legal limit on the amount of debt the U.S. Treasury is authorized to issue.

Fiscal Year

A 12-month period used for budgeting and financial reporting. The federal fiscal year runs from October 1 through September 30.

Appropriations

Laws that provide federal agencies legal authority to spend money for specified purposes.

Category 7 Polling & Public Opinion
+

Public Opinion

The views, attitudes, or preferences held by members of the public about issues, leaders, government, or events.

Poll

A survey used to measure the opinions, preferences, or experiences of a group of people.

Sample

The group of people whose responses are collected in a poll or survey.

Representative Sample

A sample designed to reflect important characteristics of the larger population being studied.

Sample Size

The number of people whose responses are included in a poll or analysis.

Margin of Error

In many probability-based polls, a statistical estimate of sampling uncertainty around a reported result.

Weighting

A statistical adjustment that changes how much certain responses contribute to the final results.

Methodology

The methods used to conduct a poll, including how participants were selected, how questions were asked, and how results were calculated.

Undecided

A respondent who has not chosen between the available options at the time of the poll.

Likely Voter

A person a pollster estimates is likely to participate in an upcoming election based on the pollster's screening or modeling method.

Response Rate

A measure of how many eligible or contacted people completed a survey compared with the number approached under the survey method.

Self-Selected Poll

A poll in which people choose for themselves whether to participate. Its participants may not represent the broader population.

Category 8 State & Local Government
+

State Government

The government of an individual state, with powers and responsibilities provided by the U.S. Constitution, the state's constitution, and state law.

Governor

The chief executive of a state government.

State Legislature

A state's lawmaking body. Most states have two legislative chambers.

Local Government

Government operating below the state level, such as counties, cities, towns, townships, or other local units.

Municipality

A local governmental unit, commonly a city or town.

County

A geographic and governmental division within a state. County responsibilities vary by state.

Mayor

An elected or appointed leader of a municipal government. The mayor's powers vary by location.

City Council

A local legislative body that can make decisions and adopt laws or ordinances for a municipality.

Ordinance

A law or rule adopted by a local government under its legal authority.

School Board

A governing body responsible for certain policies and oversight of a local public school system. Its authority varies by state and locality.

Zoning

Local rules governing how land and property may be used or developed.

Public Hearing

A government meeting or proceeding where members of the public may provide comments or testimony about a proposed action or issue.

Understanding the Language

Context Matters

Civic terms can sometimes have specific legal, governmental, or statistical meanings that are different from how the same words are used in everyday conversation.

Legal Context Some Words Have Specific Legal Meanings
+

Words such as due process, impeachment, veto, appropriation, and constitutional describe specific government or legal concepts.

Their meaning should be understood in the context of the Constitution, laws, court decisions, or government procedures involved.

Political Discussion Facts and Political Labels Are Different
+

Political discussions often include labels, slogans, opinions, and arguments in addition to factual government terminology.

A useful first question is whether a word describes a specific government process or whether it is being used to express a political viewpoint.

Context The Same Term Can Be Used Differently
+

Government responsibilities and terminology can vary between federal, state, and local governments.

For example, the powers of mayors, counties, school boards, and other local institutions are not identical everywhere.

When the exact legal meaning matters, the relevant Constitution, law, regulation, court decision, or official government source should be consulted.

The Big Picture

Government is easier to understand when the language is clear.

You do not need to memorize every civic term.

When you encounter an unfamiliar word, find out what it means, what government process it refers to, and what context is important.

Understanding the vocabulary makes it easier to understand elections, laws, government decisions, public debates, and the information you encounter every day.
