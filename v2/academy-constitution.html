Understand Your Government

The Constitution — In Simple Terms

The Constitution is the basic rulebook for the United States government. It explains how the government is organized, what powers it has, and what limits are placed on those powers.

This guide explains the Constitution in everyday language so you can understand what it means and why it matters to you.

Start Here

What is the Constitution?

In simple terms

The Constitution is the highest set of rules for the federal government.

It creates the main parts of government, explains what they are allowed to do, and sets limits on government power.

Laws passed by Congress, actions taken by the President, and decisions made by government officials must follow the Constitution.

Why does it matter?

Without basic rules, government power could be used without clear limits.

The Constitution creates a system where power is divided, officials have different responsibilities, and no single part of the federal government is supposed to control everything.

The Big Idea

Where does government power come from?

The people

The Constitution begins with the words “We the People.”

The basic idea is that government gets its authority from the people, not from a king, ruler, or permanent ruling class.

Citizens choose representatives through elections, and those officials are expected to govern under the rules of the Constitution.

Sharing Power

Why are there three branches of government?

The Constitution divides the federal government into three branches. Each branch has a different job.

Legislative Branch

Congress

Congress makes federal laws.

Congress has two parts: the House of Representatives and the Senate.

Executive Branch

The President

The executive branch carries out and enforces federal laws.

The President leads the executive branch, along with federal departments and agencies.

Judicial Branch

The Federal Courts

Federal courts decide cases involving federal law and the Constitution.

The Supreme Court is the highest court in the federal court system.

Checks and Balances

Why can't one branch control everything?

Each branch can limit the others

The Constitution gives different powers to different branches. This helps prevent too much federal power from being concentrated in one place.

This system is commonly called checks and balances.

Simple Example

Congress passes a bill

Congress can pass a bill and send it to the President.

The President can sign the bill into law or veto it.

Congress can override a presidential veto if two-thirds of both the House and Senate vote to do so.

Courts can also decide, in cases properly before them, whether government actions or laws are consistent with the Constitution.

Why does this matter to you?

Major government decisions usually involve more than one person or institution.

Dividing power makes it harder for one branch to exercise unlimited authority and creates ways for government decisions to be challenged, reviewed, or stopped.

Federal and State Power

Who controls what?

The Constitution does not give every government the same powers. Some powers belong to the federal government, while many others belong to the states.

Federal Government

National responsibilities

The Constitution gives the federal government specific powers.

These include powers involving national defense, foreign relations, interstate and international commerce, federal taxes, and money.

State Governments

State responsibilities

States have broad authority over many matters that affect people in their daily lives.

Examples include many rules involving schools, elections, public safety, property, and state and local government.

Shared Responsibilities

Sometimes both are involved

Federal and state governments sometimes work in the same areas.

Transportation, health programs, environmental rules, taxes, and emergency response can involve more than one level of government.

In simple terms

The federal government does not automatically control every government decision in the country.

The Constitution divides authority between the federal government and the states. The exact boundary can sometimes become a legal or political question.

Limits on Government

Does the Constitution limit government power?

Yes.

One of the Constitution's most important jobs is establishing rules and limits for government.

Government officials cannot simply exercise any power they want. Their actions must have legal authority and must follow constitutional requirements.

Your rights also place limits on government

The Constitution and its amendments protect important individual rights and freedoms.

These include protections involving speech, religion, due process, equal protection, criminal proceedings, voting, and other areas.

These protections are important, but constitutional rights can involve limits, exceptions, and questions about how they apply in particular situations.

Everyday Example

The Constitution is not just about Washington

Constitutional rules can affect what governments may do in schools, elections, criminal cases, protests, public meetings, and many other situations.

That is why understanding the Constitution can help you understand decisions that affect your everyday life.

Your Rights

What is the Bill of Rights?

The first 10 amendments

The Bill of Rights is the name given to the first 10 amendments to the Constitution.

These amendments protect important freedoms and legal rights and place additional limits on government power.

They cover subjects such as freedom of speech and religion, protections for people accused of crimes, and other individual rights.

What is an amendment?

An amendment is an official change or addition to the Constitution.

The Constitution has been amended 27 times.

The first 10 amendments are called the Bill of Rights. The other amendments address subjects including slavery, citizenship, voting rights, presidential terms, and other important parts of American government.

Changing the Constitution

Can the Constitution be changed?

Yes — but it is intentionally difficult.

The Constitution includes a process for proposing and approving amendments.

The most common method begins when two-thirds of both the House of Representatives and the Senate propose an amendment.

The proposed amendment then must be approved by three-fourths of the states before it becomes part of the Constitution.

Why make it difficult?

Changing the nation's highest rules requires broad agreement

A normal federal law can be changed through the regular lawmaking process.

Changing the Constitution requires much more agreement across Congress and the states.

This makes constitutional changes uncommon, but still possible when enough of the country agrees that a change should be made.

The Big Picture

What should you remember?

You do not need to memorize the Constitution to understand its basic purpose.

01

Government has rules

The Constitution creates the basic framework for the federal government and establishes rules that government officials must follow.

02

Power is divided

Federal power is divided among Congress, the President, and the federal courts so that no single branch controls everything.

03

Federal power has limits

The federal government has powers given to it by the Constitution, while states retain important powers of their own.

04

Your rights matter

The Constitution and its amendments protect important individual rights and place limits on what government may do.

05

The Constitution can change

Amendments allow the Constitution to change, but the process requires broad agreement before a change becomes part of the nation's highest law.

The simplest way to think about it

The Constitution sets the rules for government.

It creates the federal government, divides its power, limits that power, protects important rights, and provides a way to change the Constitution when enough of the country agrees.
